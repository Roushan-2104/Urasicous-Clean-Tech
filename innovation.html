Innovation

Two ideas make this grid more than panels and a battery. The array shares its land with crops, and the controller checks tomorrow's weather before deciding how much charge to keep.

Innovation 1Panels above, crops below

Innovation spotlight: . Farmland is precious in Rampur Kheda, so the grid doesn't take any of it. Our 40 kWp array stands on tall mounts about 3 m off the ground, and shade-loving crops like leafy greens and turmeric grow right underneath. One field, two harvests: electricity and food.

  • Zero land waste

    The same plot grows food and makes electricity. No family has to give up a field for the grid, and the tall mounts leave plenty of room to walk, weed and harvest between the rows.

  • Cooler panels, more power

    Solar panels lose roughly 0.4% of their output for every degree they heat up. Growing plants release moisture, so the air under the array stays cooler than bare, sun-baked ground, and the panels make more electricity on hot days.

  • A second income for farmers

    Farmers who tend the plot keep what it grows. Partial shade suits leafy greens and turmeric, and they need less water out of the harsh midday sun, so the land under the panels earns money too.

Innovation 2A battery that checks the weather

A normal controller only reacts to what is happening right now. With AI Weather Forecast Mode switched on, ours plans a day ahead, so a cloudy morning never catches the village with an empty battery.

  1. 1

    Reads the forecast

    It checks how much sun tomorrow is likely to bring.

  2. 2

    Works out what the cuts will need

    It adds up the power the village will draw during every grid cut in the next 24 hours, minus what the panels should make.

  3. 3

    Holds that much in reserve

    While the main grid is on, it saves that charge instead of spending it. Before a cloudy day it stops selling power back and tops the battery up.
