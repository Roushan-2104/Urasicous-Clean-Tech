The Rampur Kheda Initiative

Rampur Kheda is a farming village 38 km from Nashik: 2,400 people in 480 households, growing wheat and cotton, running a dairy, a flour mill and a few shops. Families fall back on diesel generators during cuts that stretch to 8 hours in summer. Our first phase brings 50 households onto a community-owned microgrid, built to scale out to the whole village.

How we sized it

We logged what five typical households run during a power cut, then scaled the average to 50 homes and added 20% for street lights and the shared water pump.

Average household need3,144 Wh / day
50 households157.2 kWh / day
+20% community loads31.44 kWh / day
Village requirement188.64 kWh / day

At 5.5 peak sun hours and 80% system efficiency, that calls for about 43 kW of panels. We specify 40 kWp of modules and let the battery bank carry the evening peak.

Generate, store, distribute

Power moves one way through the system: from the array, through the power house, out to every meter. Open each stage to see the hardware we specify.

Generate

Generate40 kWp solar array

Eighty 500 W monocrystalline modules on ground mounts, in series-parallel strings, tilted 15° to the south for year-round capture.

  • 80 × 500 W high-efficiency panels
  • About 176 kWh on a clear day at 5.5 sun hours
  • DC cabling run to the central power house
Store

Store90 kWh LiFePO4 BESS + MGCC

A lithium iron phosphate battery bank holds the midday surplus for the evening and every grid cut. The Microgrid Central Controller decides when to charge, discharge, island and export.

  • Round-trip efficiency above 92%
  • 6,000+ cycles at 80% depth of discharge
  • Integrated BMS, switch-over under 20 ms
Distribute

DistributeCables, meters and protection

Armoured cables run underground and on poles from the power house to every lane. Each home gets its own smart prepaid meter, MCB and ELCB, with lightning arresters and earth pits across the network.

  • Armoured underground and overhead lines
  • 50 smart prepaid meters, one per home
  • MCBs, ELCBs and a full earthing system

Run by the village

The grid belongs to the households it serves. A Village Electricity Committee runs it, collects fees and handles faults, with a professional service contract behind them.

The committee

  • ChairpersonGuides strategy and community coordination.
  • TreasurerHandles fees and keeps the public account books.
  • Lead technicianRuns weekly checks on the panels and batteries.
  • Two village representativesSpeak directly for the households.

Elected openly every two years by show of hands at the village assembly. Fees are a flat monthly maintenance charge tiered by usage, collected in the first week.

What happens during the monsoon or on cloudy days?

The battery keeps a reserve that bridges low-generation days, and monocrystalline panels still harvest diffuse light under cloud. On long grey spells the grid connection tops the battery up when it is available.

What if households delay or fail to pay?

Smart prepaid meters pause supply when credit runs out, so no debt builds up. The committee handles hardship cases through open, peer-to-peer accountability.

Who fixes breakdowns?

A trained local technician runs weekly checks. Major components are covered by an annual maintenance contract with a professional service provider.
